PDF Pipeline Review

A critical check of the PDF suggestions from your network: the six-stage processing diagram and the list of 70 PDF problems with proposed solutions. Each item is compared with what the engine does today, checked in the code and against the large-file test, and rated for how much it matters for our documents (vendor brochures, catalogues, news and analysis).

Are we tackling all of them?

No, and not all of them should be tackled now. The engine already covers the items that decide accuracy on our documents: no full-text prompts, structure before extraction, products assigned to sections and table rows, tables continued across pages, units from table headers, conditions, news and procurement filtering, completeness auditing and dynamic specifications. The gaps that matter are concentrated in the first three stages (page routing, figures, layout of spec cards, text clean-up) and in entity discovery on large catalogues. Handwriting, redaction, equations, fold-outs, watermarks, encryption and poor scans are rare in this corpus and can wait.

The six-stage diagram against our pipeline

We already have all six stages; no new architecture is needed. The diagram is a useful checklist for stages 1–3, which are our weakest. Two corrections to it: stages 4 and 5 are not a straight line (products in stage 4 need the entities from stage 5, so we run entities, roles and then the product map), and accuracy is decided after stage 6, in attribution and verification, which the diagram does not show. It also has no measurement step; we keep one (answer keys by position in the document).

Relevance is judged for our corpus: 250 PDFs, 2,203 pages, of which 3.1% have no text layer and 11.3% are mostly image; most files are vendor brochures and catalogues, not scans, tenders or government documents.

What we learn from the list

  • It confirms the direction already taken: structure first, products bound to sections and table cells, evidence for every value, dynamic specifications, and a cheap-to-expensive cascade.
  • It names three gaps our tests also found: figures on text pages are dropped, spec cards with labels and numbers in separate boxes lose values (Lapua: 12/26), and page routing is partly per document instead of per page.
  • It adds risks we had not tested: tender documents (requirements read as specifications), bad text layers, OCR digit errors, contents pages and sidebars.
  • One recommendation does not fit our goal: retrieving only the relevant passages (items 1, 67) suits question answering, but exhaustive extraction must read every chunk, or it misses specifications. We keep exhaustive reading, densest chunks first.
  • The list says how to fix things but not how to know they are fixed. Every item we take on gets a test document and a check in the answer keys.

Changes to the framework

Now: highest effect on accuracy

  1. Catalogue entities. Model-number headings and table row labels become products before roles are assigned (items 21, 22). Copper-alloy catalogue today: 16–22 of 55.
  2. Spec cards. Join label, unit and number boxes into one field using their positions (items 2, 37, 38). Lapua today: 12 of 26.
  3. Figures. Keep figure regions, bind captions, transcribe figures that carry technical labels (items 7, 8, 26).
  4. Per-page routing. A text-quality score per page decides native text, OCR or vision (items 3, 4, 56).
  5. Text clean-up. Dehyphenation, paragraph continuation across pages, contents and index marked as navigation (items 14, 15, 50).
  6. Roles. Entities the role model calls "other" get the role their type implies (item 22).

Next: after the bigger evaluation set

  1. Requirement vs product-specification role for tenders (46).
  2. Visual confirmation of low-confidence and image-read numbers (53, 66).
  3. Bounding box and table cell in every evidence item (65).
  4. Acronym dictionary, footnote conditions, version and upgrade conditions (19, 18, 40, 44).
  5. Sidebars, common variant columns, multi-row headers (27, 23, 38).
  6. Explicit status for encrypted or corrupt files; page-window isolation (58, 59).

Deferred until such documents appear: watermarks, rotated text, fold-outs, poor scans, handwriting, redaction, classification banners, equations, charts, reference graphs (12, 28, 30, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 10, 17).

How we raise accuracy

Where the errors are today

  • Large files: 77.8% of sampled specs found, 5.4% of found values on a wrong product; middle of documents 67%, start 83%, end 78%.
  • Three documents carry most of the misses: the copper-alloy catalogue (entity discovery), Lapua (spec cards) and a long news log (roles labelled "other").
  • 14 of the 20 wrong-product values were dynamic specifications.

What moves the number

  • Fix the three error sources above first (changes 1, 2 and 6).
  • Give the verifier an independent model: the farm's 72B once it has a large enough context and speed (now every verifier call falls back to the 27B).
  • Use light thinking from the start for entity finding on big PDFs (47% of those calls came back empty with thinking off).
  • Measure every change on the benchmark, the corpus keys and the large-file keys, by position in the document.

Next steps and blockers

Plan

  1. Re-run the benchmark on the farm to confirm the switch from OpenRouter at full scale (the two-page smoke test matched: 22/23 on both).
  2. Build the six "now" changes, each with a unit test and a test document.
  3. Re-run the 15 large documents on the farm and compare by position.
  4. Fold in your bigger evaluation set, then take the "next" changes.

Blockers

  • Farm throughput. qwen3.8:27b serves one request at a time at about 40 tokens/s: about 6–7 minutes per small page, days for a 500-document chunk. Needs batching (vLLM or SGLang, or more llama.cpp slots) and more replicas.
  • 72B not usable yet. qwen2.5-72b-instruct is served with a 2,048-token context window, smaller than any audit or verification prompt (5,000–15,000 tokens), and answers at about 0.5 tokens/s. Every verifier call falls back to the 27B until the farm serves it with a 32k context on enough GPU memory.
  • Thinking cannot be switched off on the farm, which adds reasoning tokens to every call. The server needs to honour chat_template_kwargs.
  • Proxy timeout of 95 s. Handled by streaming; raising it to 15 minutes would also protect queued requests.
  • Evaluation set. The current keys sample 446 specs on 15 large documents; the bigger set will narrow the error bars.
  • Code review. Pull request #2 on ScriptXDemon/Ex_Poc waits for the owner to merge.
